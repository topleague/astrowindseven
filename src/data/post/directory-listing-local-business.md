If your customers come from a particular town or city, local search is probably the most valuable kind of SEO for you. Someone searching "dentist near me" or "wedding photographer in Cuttack" is usually ready to act, and Google answers with a map and a short list of businesses before it shows ordinary website results.

Getting into that list depends on a few things you can control. This guide covers them in order of importance.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile (the free listing that used to be called Google My Business) is what appears in Google Maps and in the local panel of search results. For most local businesses it matters more than any directory.

To set it up well:

- Claim or create your profile at business.google.com and complete Google's verification. The method varies by business and country.
- Choose the primary category that best describes what you do. It carries a lot of weight, so be specific. Add secondary categories only where they are true.
- Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your signboard. Adding keywords or locations to the name is against Google's guidelines and can get a listing suspended.
- Add accurate opening hours, including holidays, and keep them updated.
- List your services or products, with short honest descriptions.
- Add real photos of your premises, team and work.
- Set your service area if you travel to customers.
- Link to the most relevant page of your website, which is often your home page or a location page.

Google's own help pages at support.google.com/business explain the current rules and options, and they change from time to time, so check them when you set up.

## Ask for reviews and answer them

Reviews influence both rankings and whether someone picks you. Ask satisfied customers to leave one, perhaps with a short message after a job is done and a link to your review page. Do not offer rewards for reviews, do not write your own, and do not ask only happy customers in a way that looks staged. Google's policies prohibit fake and incentivised reviews.

Reply to reviews, good and bad, politely and without arguing. A calm answer to a complaint tells future customers more about you than the complaint does.

## Keep your name, address and phone number consistent

Search engines cross-check your business details across the web. These three items are often called NAP: name, address and phone number. If your website says "Sunrise Dental Clinic, Plot 12" and a directory says "Sunrise Dentals, 12 Main Road", it creates doubt about which is correct.

Choose one exact form of your name, address (including PIN or ZIP code) and phone number, and use it everywhere:

- Your website's contact page and footer
- Your Google Business Profile
- Every directory and social profile

Also remove duplicates. They usually appear when two people created listings at different times, or when you moved and the old listing was never closed. Search for your business name and phone number, and claim or request removal of duplicates and old addresses.

## Choose directories carefully

In the past, signing up on dozens of directories was standard advice. Today it is the quality of a listing, not the count, that matters. Most free directories send no visitors and have no influence on rankings. A few are worth the effort:

- **Maps and search platforms**: Google Business Profile, Bing Places for Business and Apple Business Connect. These are the sources many other services draw from.
- **Industry directories** your customers actually use, for example a medical association's listing, a travel review site for hotels, or a marketplace for trades.
- **Local directories and chambers of commerce** in your own city or region. In India, the well-known local listing sites in your category are worth checking, but only if they are real and active.
- **Social profiles**, such as a Facebook page or LinkedIn company page, using the same business details.

A good test is whether you would be happy for a customer to find you there. If a directory looks abandoned, is full of spam, or asks you to pay for a "ranking boost", skip it.

Be cautious of tools and listings named in older guides. Some services have closed, been merged or become US-only, and you should check any tool's coverage before you rely on it. For example, tools built to audit US citations may show nothing for an Indian business.

## Support your listing with your website

Local rankings also reflect your site. Include your address and phone number as text on the contact page, make sure the site works well on mobile, and add a page for each location you serve. If you have several branches, give each its own page with its own details. ## What to expect

A complete, consistent profile and a steady flow of genuine reviews often brings a visible improvement in local results within weeks to a few months, depending on competition. The work is not complicated, but it needs regular attention: update hours, post offers, respond to reviews and fix details when anything changes.
