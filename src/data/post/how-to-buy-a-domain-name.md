A domain name is your website's address, such as yourbusiness.com. It is the first thing to sort out when you start a website, because everything else, from your email addresses to your business cards, will carry it.

This is part 1 of a three-part beginner series. Part 2 covers [choosing web hosting](/web-hosting-service-guide/) and part 3 covers [choosing a CMS](/cms-wordpress-joomla-drupal/). None of it needs technical knowledge.

## What buying a domain actually means

You do not buy a domain permanently. You register it for a period, usually one year or more, and renew it to keep it. You register through a domain registrar, a company accredited to sell domains, or through a reseller of one. If you use a reseller, find out which registrar is behind it and who holds the account.

Whichever you choose, check a few things before you pay:

- The domain is registered in **your** name or your company's, and you hold the login. Agencies and freelancers sometimes register domains in their own name, which can lead to disputes later.
- The **renewal price**. Introductory prices are often low and the second-year price can be much higher. Look at the renewal rate before you buy.
- The registrar supports **two-factor authentication**, a transfer lock and auto-renew.
- The contact email on the account is one you will keep reading. Expiry warnings go there.

If you are a beginner, buying the domain from a reputable registrar and keeping it separate from your hosting also makes it easier to change hosts later. Many beginners buy both from one company for convenience, and that is fine if you remember that you can move either one.

## How to choose a good name

Finding a name is the hard part, because most short, obvious names were registered years ago. These guidelines help:

- **Keep it short and easy to say.** If you have to spell it out on the phone, it is too complicated. Shorter names are easier to remember and type correctly.
- **Make it easy to spell.** Avoid unusual spellings and words people mix up.
- **Avoid hyphens and numbers.** They look untidy and are easy to get wrong when said aloud. A name like best-web-designer-2.com does not inspire trust.
- **Think about your brand first.** A brandable name, one that is distinct and can sit on a signboard or invoice, is a better long-term choice than a string of keywords.
- **Check for clashes.** Search for the name on Google and on social media. If a similar business already operates under it, choose another. Also check that you are not infringing someone's trademark.
- **Say it aloud, and type it without spaces.** Read the combined letters to make sure they do not form an unfortunate word.

### Keywords in the name

Years ago, a domain made of search words, like "cheapdelhihotels.com", could help rankings. That advantage has largely gone, and Google does not need your domain to contain your keywords. A name that matches your business and is easy to remember is a better investment. You can use keywords on the pages themselves; see our [keyword research guide](/keyword-research/).

## Choosing a domain extension

The ending, such as .com or .in, is called a top-level domain (TLD).

- **.com** is still the most familiar to the public and a safe default, especially if you want customers in several countries.
- **A country extension** such as .in (India) or .co.in signals that you serve that country, and may be easier to find available. For a business that serves mainly Indian customers, .in is a good and credible choice.
- **.org** suits non-profits and communities; .net is a fallback for technology companies.
- **Newer extensions** such as .shop, .studio or .agency can be fine if they fit the business and you use them consistently, but they are less familiar to some visitors, who may type .com by mistake.
- Be cautious with cheap or obscure extensions. They are often cheap because of heavy spam use, and some email providers treat mail from them with suspicion.

If the .com is taken by an unrelated business, consider a different name rather than an awkward variation. If you plan to use two extensions, point the second to the first so visitors reach the same site.

## Domain privacy

When you register a domain, you must give your name, address, phone number and email. Registrars have long published these in a public database called WHOIS. This led to spam and scam calls. Today many registrars hide or redact personal details by default, and others offer a "WHOIS privacy" or "domain privacy" add-on, sometimes free and sometimes paid.

Check what your registrar does. Where privacy is offered, it is worth having if you are registering as an individual. Your details must still be accurate in the registrar's records; privacy only hides them from the public.

## After you buy

- Turn on auto-renew, and set a calendar reminder for the expiry date in any case. Losing a domain through an expired card is a painful and avoidable mistake.
- Keep your account login and recovery email somewhere safe, with a second person able to find them in an emergency.
- Register obvious variants only if they matter to you, for instance a common misspelling.
- Do not pay for unsolicited "domain renewal" notices that arrive by post or email from companies you do not know. They are a common scam; renew only through your own registrar.
