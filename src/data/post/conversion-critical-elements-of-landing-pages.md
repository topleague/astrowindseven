A landing page is the page a visitor arrives on after clicking an advert, a search result or an email link. Unlike a home page, it is built around a single action: request a quote, book a call, download a guide or buy a product.

If you pay for clicks and few visitors act, the problem is usually the page, not the traffic. Think of the visitor as someone who has just arrived at your shop door with a specific expectation. Everything on the page should confirm they are in the right place and make the next step easy.

## The elements that matter

### A headline that matches the promise

The headline is the first thing people read. It should repeat the promise that brought them there. If your advert says "Office desks under Rs 15,000", the page should open with that, not a general "Welcome to our furniture store". A mismatch makes visitors doubt they clicked the right thing, and they leave.

### A supporting line that speaks to their problem

Beneath the headline, a short secondary line can address the reader's real concern. For a desk seller it might be "Sturdy desks for small offices that do not stretch the budget". Say what you do, who it is for and why it helps, in plain words.

### Clear, error-free writing

A landing page is a trust-sensitive page. The visitor does not know you yet, and spelling or grammar mistakes make them wonder what else is careless. Read the page aloud, have someone else proofread it, and keep sentences short. Specific details beat vague claims: say "delivery in three working days" if that is true, not "fast delivery".

### Proof that others trust you

Testimonials, ratings, client logos, certifications, years in business and secure payment badges reduce doubt. Use only real ones, and attach a name and detail where the customer agrees. A short, specific testimonial ("Installed in a day and the team cleaned up after") works better than "Great service!". Never invent or exaggerate social proof.

### One strong call to action

Tell people exactly what to do next, using a verb: "Get a free quote", "Book a demo", "Download the checklist". Say what happens after the click if it is not obvious. Avoid vague labels such as "Submit". If the page has a form, ask only for what you need. Every extra field loses some people.

### A button that stands out

The call-to-action button should be easy to spot. Use a colour that contrasts with the background and the rest of the page, with enough size and spacing to tap comfortably on a phone. A bright button that blends into a bright banner does not help.

### A single focus

Remove anything that distracts from the goal. A full navigation menu, a sidebar and many outbound links give visitors reasons to wander. Many landing pages have a logo and one action only. Repeat the same call to action at sensible points down a longer page rather than offering different ones.

### Images and video that explain

Show the product or service in use, or the outcome. A short video or a clear set of photos can explain more than a paragraph. Use your own photos where you can, and keep files small so the page loads fast. Add alt text for accessibility.

### The important message in view without scrolling

Keep the headline, the main benefit and the call to action visible when the page first loads, on both desktop and phone. This does not mean packing everything in. Visitors do scroll, but they decide quickly whether to stay.

## Four mistakes that stop people converting

Even a good page loses people when the basics are missing.

1. **Hard to get in touch.** If visitors have to hunt for a way to contact you, many will not. Show a phone number, email or short enquiry form on service pages, not only on a separate contact page. A simple form in a WordPress site is easy to add with any reputable forms plugin.
2. **A contact page that is hard to find.** Link it in the main menu and the footer. If it is not visible, for the visitor it does not exist.
3. **No empathy.** Visitors arrive with questions. Answer the likely ones near the action: price range, delivery or turnaround, how the process works. If someone could not find what they wanted, give them an obvious route such as a phone number or chat.
4. **No way to try before committing.** Buyers are right to be cautious. Where it makes sense for your business, offer a free consultation, sample, demo, trial or money-back guarantee, and state the terms plainly.

## Check, measure and improve

Test on a real phone as well as a laptop; most visitors are likely to use phones. Make sure the page loads quickly and the form actually works. Then track how many visitors take the action, change one thing at a time, and compare. Rules of thumb are a starting point; your audience will tell you what works.
