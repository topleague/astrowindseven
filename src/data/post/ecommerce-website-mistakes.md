Nobody opens a shop in a mall and leaves out the shelves. Yet many online shops launch with missing product details, a checkout nobody tested and a layout that breaks on a phone. Visitors do not complain. They leave, and you never find out why.

We have built and reviewed enough small business stores to see the same mistakes repeat. Here are the ones that cost the most, and what to do about each.

## 1. Missing or copied product descriptions

Many shops either leave descriptions empty or paste the manufacturer's text. Both hurt. A buyer cannot touch the product, so the description is the only salesperson. Copied text also means dozens of other shops show identical words, which gives search engines no reason to prefer yours.

Write for each product: what it is, who it suits, size, materials, what is included, how to use or care for it, and the questions customers ask most. Include the detail a returns policy would otherwise have to settle. If you have hundreds of products, start with your best sellers and the pages that already get visits. Our guide to [product descriptions that sell](/product-descriptions-that-sell/) covers the method in more depth.

## 2. A site that is poor on mobile

Most online shopping traffic in India comes from phones, and in many other markets it is the majority too. A design that merely shrinks to fit is not enough. Check that:

- product images load quickly and are sized for small screens
- buttons and form fields are large enough to tap
- filters, the cart and checkout work with one thumb
- pages do not jump around as they load

Test on a real mid-range phone over an ordinary mobile connection, not only on your office Wi-Fi. Google's Core Web Vitals reports in Search Console show how real visitors experience your loading speed, responsiveness and layout stability. Heavy sliders, uncompressed images and too many plugins are the usual causes of slow stores.

## 3. Weak customer support

Even a well-built store leaves questions: does this fit, when will it arrive, what if I want to return it? A buyer who cannot get an answer quickly often buys elsewhere.

You do not need a large team. You need the basics in plain sight:

- a clear delivery, returns and refund policy, linked from product pages and checkout
- visible contact details, including a phone number or WhatsApp where your customers expect it
- an FAQ built from questions customers actually ask
- live chat or a messaging option, only if someone will answer it. An unanswered chat box is worse than none

Reply time matters as much as the channel. Decide what you can honestly promise and state it.

## 4. Design that undermines trust

Design on a shop is not decoration. Inconsistent fonts, blurry photos, cluttered menus and unclear prices make a store look unreliable, and people do not enter card details on a site that looks unreliable. We cannot point to a published statement that Google ranks sites on how attractive they are, so we will not claim that. What is well established is that poor design reduces conversions, and slow, unstable pages do show up in Google's page experience signals.

Focus on the practical parts: clear category navigation, working search, breadcrumbs, consistent product photos, visible prices and delivery costs, and trust signals such as real reviews, secure payment badges only where genuinely applicable, and clear company details.

## 5. Checkout friction

Ask how many steps and fields stand between "add to cart" and "paid". Forcing account creation, hiding shipping costs until the last step and asking for information you do not need are common causes of abandoned carts. Offer guest checkout, show the total cost early, and support the payment methods your customers use. For Indian customers that usually includes UPI and cash on delivery where it makes sense. For US customers, major cards and wallets are the norm.

## 6. A payment system nobody monitors

Payment gateways fail quietly. A client of ours once saw a sudden rise in abandoned carts and found that card payments had stopped working after a gateway change. The fix took a short time, but the sales lost in between were gone.

Protect yourself:

- place a real test order, with a real payment, after any plugin, theme or gateway update
- set up order and payment failure notifications
- review abandoned cart numbers weekly, and investigate sharp changes
- keep an eye on gateway status pages and your own error logs

A short weekly checklist is cheaper than discovering a broken checkout from your bank statement.

## Also worth checking

Keep the shop secure and backed up, since store sites handle customer data (our guide on [protecting your WordPress website](/protect-your-wordpress-website/) is a good start), and make sure product pages carry proper structured data so search engines can read price and availability.

## Before you launch, or before the next sale

Walk through your own shop as a first-time customer on a phone: find a product, read about it, add it, pay, and read the confirmation. Every point of confusion you hit is a lost sale for someone else.
