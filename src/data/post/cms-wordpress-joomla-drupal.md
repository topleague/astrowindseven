A CMS, or content management system, is the software that lets you create and edit your website without writing code for every page. You log in, type your text, add images, press publish, and the system builds the page. Almost every news site, blog and business site you visit runs on one.

This is part 3 of our beginner series. Part 1 explained [how to buy a domain name](/how-to-buy-a-domain-name/) and part 2 covered [choosing web hosting](/web-hosting-service-guide/). The CMS is what you install on that hosting.

## Why use a CMS at all

Before content management systems, every change meant editing code by hand. A CMS separates the content from the design. That means:

- You can add or change pages yourself, and several people can contribute.
- A theme controls the look, so you do not design every page from scratch.
- Add-ons (plugins, extensions or modules) provide forms, shops, bookings and SEO features without custom programming.
- A site built on a popular CMS is easier to hand to another developer later.

## The main options

### WordPress

WordPress began as a blogging tool in 2003 and is now the most widely used CMS in the world, behind a very large share of all websites. It is free, open source software.

Why it suits most small businesses:

- **Easy to start.** Most hosts offer one-click installation.
- **Huge ecosystem.** Thousands of themes and plugins, and a very large pool of developers, designers and tutorials. Finding someone to help is easy and usually affordable.
- **Flexible.** It handles brochure sites, blogs, online shops (with WooCommerce) and membership sites.
- **Modern editing.** The block editor lets you build pages visually. Modern block themes with Full Site Editing let you adjust headers, footers and templates in the same editor, without touching code.
- **Strong community and documentation** at wordpress.org.

Watch-outs: because it is so common, it is a target for attackers, and every plugin you add is extra code to keep updated. Choose plugins sparingly, keep everything updated, and read our [WordPress security guide](/protect-your-wordpress-website/) and [plugin advice](/wordpress-plugins/).

### Drupal

Drupal is a powerful open source CMS favoured by developers and large organisations, including government, universities and publishers, for complex sites with detailed content structures, permissions and multiple languages.

It is highly capable, but the learning curve is steeper, and you will usually need a specialist developer for changes. Finding and paying for that expertise can cost more than for WordPress. For a typical small business site, it is more than you need.

### Joomla

Joomla sits between the two in complexity. It offers strong built-in options for multilingual sites and user management, and has an active community. Its market share is much smaller than WordPress now, so there are fewer themes, extensions and developers to choose from.

### Hosted website builders

Services such as Wix, Squarespace and Shopify let you build a site inside their own platform, with hosting included. They are simple to start and need little maintenance, which suits very small sites or shops. The trade-offs are less flexibility, ongoing subscription costs, and difficulty moving your site elsewhere later, because the content is tied to the platform. Check the details of any plan before committing.

## How to decide

Ask yourself these questions:

1. **Who will update the site?** If you or a non-technical colleague, pick something with a friendly editor.
2. **What does the site need to do?** A few pages and a contact form, or bookings, a shop, a members area?
3. **What is the budget, including later changes?** A popular platform gives you more developers to choose from, and so more competitive prices.
4. **How long will it last?** Choose software that is actively maintained and likely to be around in five years.
5. **Can you leave?** Prefer systems where you own the files and content, and can move to another host.

For most small and medium businesses, the answer is WordPress. If you have a technical team and an unusual requirement, Drupal or a custom build may be a better fit. If you only need a tiny site quickly, a hosted builder may do.

## After you choose

- Pick a lightweight, well-maintained theme. Fast, simple themes tend to beat heavy multipurpose ones.
- Install only the plugins you need and remove those you stop using.
- Set up HTTPS, backups and automatic security updates from day one.
- Plan your page structure and content before design; our [blogging strategy guide](/blogging-strategy/) can help you decide what to publish.
- Set up your pages for search from the start; our [guide to getting found on Google](/how-to-get-your-website-listed-on-googles-first-page/) explains the basics.
