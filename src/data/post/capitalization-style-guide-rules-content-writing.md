Capitalization looks like a small matter, yet it is one of the most common sources of inconsistency on business websites. One page uses Title Case For Every Heading, the next uses sentence case, and a third capitalizes random words for emphasis. Readers may not name the problem, but the site feels less careful.

The goal is not to find the single correct rule. It is to pick one style, write it down, and apply it everywhere.

## Two styles, and when each fits

**Title case** capitalizes the important words in a heading: "How to Choose a Web Hosting Plan for Your Business". It looks formal and is common for article titles, book titles and news headlines in many publications.

**Sentence case** capitalizes only the first word and proper nouns: "How to choose a web hosting plan for your business". It reads as more conversational, is easier to apply correctly, and is the default in most modern web and app interfaces.

Neither is wrong. Which suits you depends on your brand and design. A formal consultancy may prefer title case; a friendly local service may prefer sentence case. There is no known ranking benefit to either, so choose for readability and consistency.

## The usual rules for title case

Style guides disagree on the details (AP, Chicago, APA and MLA each differ), but most follow this core:

- Capitalize the first and last word, whatever they are.
- Capitalize nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs.
- Capitalize subordinating conjunctions such as "because", "although" and "when".
- Lowercase articles: "a", "an", "the".
- Lowercase coordinating conjunctions: "and", "but", "or", "nor".
- Lowercase short prepositions such as "in", "on", "at", "to", "of" and "with".

Where guides differ most is on longer prepositions and conjunctions. Some capitalize any word of four or more letters ("With", "From", "Between"), while others lowercase all prepositions regardless of length. The word "to" in an infinitive is usually lowercase in Chicago style, but some guides treat it differently. If you have no house style, choose one major guide and follow its rule for these edge cases.

Also decide on these in advance:

- **Hyphenated words.** "Step-by-Step Guide" or "Step-by-step Guide"? Pick one.
- **Words after a colon.** Many guides capitalize the first word after a colon in a title.
- **Acronyms and brand names.** Keep them as the owner writes them: SEO, WordPress, iPhone, eBay.
- **Numbers.** Decide whether you write "5 ways" or "Five ways" in headings.

## Subheadings

Many sites use title case for the main title and sentence case for subheadings. That is a sensible split: the title is a formal label, and subheadings read more like part of the sentence flow. Whatever you choose:

- Use the same style for every subheading at the same level.
- Skip end punctuation, except a question mark when the subheading is a question.
- Keep each subheading short enough to scan.

## Single-author and multi-author sites

On a site written by one person, consistency is simply a habit. On a site with several writers, differences multiply quickly, and the editor ends up fixing them by hand. A one-page style sheet solves most of this. It should state:

1. Title case or sentence case for page titles
2. The style for subheadings
3. Spelling variant: US, UK or Indian English, applied the same way everywhere
4. How to write numbers, dates and currency
5. Your list of brand names and product names, spelled and capitalized as they should appear

Because our clients write for readers in both India and the USA, we also recommend choosing one spelling convention per site and sticking to it, rather than mixing "optimize" and "optimise" on the same page.

## Check as you edit

Most word processors and editors can change case automatically, but their title case is often only approximate and misses the edge cases above. Treat automated tools as a first pass and read the result yourself. Pay particular attention to proper nouns, acronyms and words after colons, which tools frequently get wrong.

Remember that headlines appear in places beyond the page: search results, social previews, browser tabs and emails. A consistent style carries through all of them.

A written style sheet is one of the simplest ways to [improve your content quality](/improve-content-writing-quality/) across a whole site.

## In short

Choose title case or sentence case, pick one style guide for the edge cases, write it down, and apply it to every heading. A style sheet takes less than an hour to write and saves far more than that in editing.
