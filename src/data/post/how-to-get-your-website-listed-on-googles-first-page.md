When a potential customer types "best hotel in Bhubaneswar" or "accountant near me" into Google, they see a page of results within a fraction of a second. If your business has a website, the natural question is why some sites appear near the top and yours does not appear at all.

There is no secret formula, and nobody outside Google can promise you a position. But the basics are well understood, and most of them are within a small business owner's reach. This guide explains what has to happen for your site to appear on Google, and what moves it up.

## Why being on Google matters to a business

Most people now start a purchase decision with a search, often on a phone. They look for a service, a shop, a price or an answer to a question. If your business is not in those results, the customer goes to the one that is. That is true even if you have a physical shop and spend on local advertising: the search result is often where people check you out before they visit or call.

SEO (search engine optimization) is simply the work of making your site easy for Google to find and understand, and worth showing to people who search. It is not a trick. It is a set of habits.

## Step 1: Make sure Google can find and index your site

Google keeps a huge catalogue of web pages, called its index. A page can only show up in results if it is in that index. Google adds pages mostly by following links from pages it already knows, so a new site with no links pointing to it can take a while to be noticed.

You can speed this up, and you do not need to pay anyone to "submit" your site:

- Create a free Google Search Console account and verify your site. Search Console is Google's own tool for site owners.
- Submit your sitemap, a file listing your pages. Most WordPress SEO plugins and many site builders generate one automatically.
- Use the URL Inspection tool in Search Console to check that your main pages are indexed and to request indexing for new ones.
- Check that you have not accidentally told Google to stay away. A "noindex" setting or a blocking robots.txt file, left over from the development stage, is a surprisingly common reason a new site never appears. In WordPress, look for the "discourage search engines" option under Settings, Reading.

Once your site is indexed, appearing is a matter of relevance and trust, not submission.

## Step 2: Write for what people actually search

Google tries to show the page that best answers a search. That means your pages need to use the words your customers use, not the words you use internally. A hotel owner may say "hospitality accommodation"; a traveller types "hotel near the airport".

Start by listing the questions and phrases customers use when they find you or call you. Keyword tools help you check how often people search for them, and our [keyword research guide](/keyword-research/) explains how to do that without special software. Then:

- Give each important service or product its own page, with a clear title and heading that says what it is.
- Write naturally and completely. Repeating a phrase many times does not help, and it makes the page unpleasant to read.
- Answer the follow-up questions a buyer would have: price range, location, how it works, what happens next.
- Make the page genuinely useful. Google's systems are built to reward content written for people, whether a person or software produced the first draft.

## Step 3: Earn links and mentions from other sites

When many websites have similar content, Google needs some other signal to choose between them. One of the strongest has long been links: when other reputable sites link to you, it suggests that others find your site worth recommending.

The aim is quality, not quantity. A link from a local newspaper, a trade association, a supplier or a respected blog in your field is worth far more than hundreds from irrelevant directories. Ways small businesses earn them honestly include:

- Publishing something worth citing, such as a clear guide, original data from your own work, or a useful tool.
- Being listed in genuine local and industry directories. See our [local SEO and Google Business Profile guide](/directory-listing-local-business/).
- Sponsoring or taking part in local events, and asking organisers to link to you.
- Getting mentioned by customers, partners and suppliers who already know you.

Buying links in bulk is risky and can do more harm than good.

## Step 4: Get the technical basics right

A site that is slow, broken on phones or insecure will struggle even with good content. Check that:

- The site uses HTTPS.
- Pages are easy to read and use on a mobile screen. Google primarily uses the mobile version of a page.
- Pages load quickly and do not jump around as they load. Google measures this through Core Web Vitals, and Search Console shows how your pages score.
- Every page can be reached through normal links from other pages on your site.

## If you serve a local area

If customers come to a shop or you serve a specific city, set up a Google Business Profile as well. It controls how your business appears in Google Maps and in the local results, and it is free.

## How long does it take?

Expect weeks before a new site is indexed and several months before steady results appear for competitive searches. Be wary of anyone who guarantees a first-page position. Nobody controls Google's results, and the honest answer depends on your competition, your content and your site.
